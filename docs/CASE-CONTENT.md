# Case study content

The reader is assessing the work and whether it is relevant to their own project.

- Lead with one plain sentence stating the site's purpose and audience. Do not invent a question for the client or turn the introduction into a pitch.
- Use the same introduction in listings and search descriptions. Do not repeat it as an extra summary on the case page.
- Keep context that explains a real constraint, integration, business problem or division of responsibility.
- Describe the delivered functions. Remove revision counts, abandoned designs, design-option anecdotes and routine implementation steps.
- Keep results only when supported by the project record. State the measurement method where it affects the meaning. Do not present lab measurements as business outcomes or claim someone else's work.
- State launch or private-preview status accurately. A completed design is not necessarily a launched site.
- Use visible media captions only when they explain something the image cannot show, such as booking-system integration or where form responses go. Keep descriptive alt text for accessibility.
- Historical comparisons belong only to genuine redesign work where they help evaluate the change. Refreshing portfolio captures does not make a project a redesign case study.

`src/content/cases.ts` is the source for case introductions, context, scope, results and curated media notes. `src/content/found.ts` and the website-check report refer to specific findings; update those references whenever a finding is removed or reworded.

KinetiKare is presented as the current educational resource and marketing site for a solo physiotherapy practice, with no historical comparisons or redesign narrative.
